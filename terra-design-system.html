The problem

The interface was competing with the work.

Maz opened over the app and covered information. It wasn’t aware of the current page, and navigating away closed it instead of keeping the conversation available. Meanwhile, a long navigation and independently built page toolbars made the product harder to learn.

I redesigned the experience and the shared components together. Navigation, global controls, page controls, and AI assistance each needed a clear home.

The experience change

From an overlay to a working companion.

I moved Maz into a collapsible panel on the right. The redesigned experience keeps the current conversation available as people navigate, understands where they are in the app, and can give the page its space back when they need it.

Information architecture

A navigation that follows the work.

The previous navigation mixed roughly 18 tools into one list. Meetings sat tenth, while reporting appeared before the work it reports on. I reorganized the structure around how teams run EOS, with Home and Meetings pinned above three groups.

Shared page architecture

One home for each kind of control.

I created a new left navigation, Global Top Bar, Page Header, and Filter Bar. The top bar holds the shared team setting, notifications, and Create. Search lives in navigation. Page headers stop repeating global controls and follow one hierarchy: title, page actions, tabs, then filters and view actions.

The header responds to its own available width, including the space left when Maz is docked—not just the size of the browser.

The reusable pieces

The details make the system usable.

The shell is supported by component work: simplifying button properties, defining compact and full-width behavior, aligning states, and documenting how components behave when space changes.

Guidance that goes beyond appearance.

The Side Navigation guidelines cover anatomy, group behavior, themes, rail flyouts, short-window scrolling, keyboard access, and update history. They also separate responsibilities: navigation holds places; the top bar and page header hold actions.

More about the component and system work

Audit actual use before defining a pattern.

I audited Storybook and live product patterns to define pagination states and behavior below 600px. Details Sheet and Bottom Sheet became separate components with a shared content slot; the layout also specifies when Maz must hide because two panels cannot fit.

Keep Figma and implementation connected.

Storybook’s Terra section is the implementation source of truth. I aligned components with existing tokens, recorded remaining Chip and Switch mismatches, and separated visual guidance from product requirements and implementation notes.

Make AI recognizable within Terra.

I proposed a dedicated purple ramp for Maz instead of inheriting each customer’s brand color. Engineering implemented the token. Its AI-only use lives in the guidelines; Terra does not yet have a semantic alias layer.

Document accessibility gaps honestly.

I corrected failing button labels and icons using existing tokens. Terra’s existing focus ring was documented as failing the 3:1 non-text contrast requirement; a consistent system-wide replacement remains outstanding.

What changed

A shared structure for the next product decision.

The redesign gives the app a consistent answer to questions that used to be resolved page by page: where a team change belongs, how filters relate to actions, and what happens to the page when Maz opens. Those decisions now live in shared components and guidance.

As the sole designer responsible for Terra, I used the shell redesign to connect the system to everyday product work. Maz’s panel exposed a layout problem; solving it meant defining how the page header, navigation, and content share space. The product experience shaped the component rules, and those rules can now support other pages.

Engineering review also changed the design. I removed the navigation’s width animation when it became clear that rows would reflow awkwardly during the transition. That was a useful tradeoff: preserve a stable reading experience rather than animate every change.

The next test is whether this structure holds up across complete workflows: finding a tool, changing pages with Maz open, and recovering space without losing access to page controls. The prototype makes those questions concrete enough to evaluate with users and engineers.
